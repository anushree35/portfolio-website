My path

How I got here.

What STEM success meant to me when I started college

I thought it was all about grades and networking. But I didn't really know what networking even meant. I just wanted to chase every new opportunity I could find.

What it means to me now

I learned that it also means going deeper than what I learn in my CS classes. I've been doing LeetCode and getting better at the things I'm supposed to be good at.

I also learned to just apply and go for it, even when I don't feel ready. That's how I've found most of my opportunities.

I'll come back to this at the end of the semester and see if it changed.

Middle school

My first project: a snake game

I built a snake game from scratch. It was the first time I made something on a computer that actually worked.

What was hardI had no idea what I was doing. Most of it was watching tutorials, breaking the code, and trying again.
What I learnedYou don't have to feel ready to start. I learned more from building one messy game than from reading about coding.
High school

Founding the SWHS Photo Club

I started my school's photography club and grew it to 30+ members.

What was hardGetting people to come back after the first meeting. Starting something is easy. Keeping it going while doing my classes was the hard part.
What I learnedLeading is mostly being consistent. When I showed up every week with a plan, people kept coming.
Read the Bobcat Prowl article about the club →
High school

Food4Health for Hartford Hospital

I built a full-stack system for Hartford Hospital's prescription food assistance program using Python, Flask, React, and PostgreSQL.

What was hardIt was my first time building something for real people at a real organization. What I thought was cool wasn't always what they actually needed.
What I learnedTalk to the people who are going to use it before you write the code. It saves a lot of rebuilding.
High school

Volunteering at Hartford Hospital

I volunteered over 200 hours at Hartford Hospital, and I earned the Bronze Presidential Volunteer Service Award in 2024.

What was hardShowing up consistently on top of school, and learning how to help people who were having a hard day.
What I learnedSeeing how the hospital actually worked is what made Food4Health make sense to me. The best projects come from understanding real problems up close.
High school

Girl Scout Gold Award

I set up a food recovery program that sends 40 to 60 pounds of food a week to local pantries.

What was hardKeeping it running every single week and coordinating with everyone involved, not just getting it started.
What I learnedA project really matters when it can keep going without you. I had to set it up so other people could run it too.
2024

Miss Teen Bharat Connecticut

I competed in Miss Teen Bharat Connecticut and placed first runner-up.

What was hardGetting up on a stage in front of a big audience and staying confident through every round.
What I learnedConfidence is something you practice. After that stage, presentations and interviews feel a lot less scary.
2024 to 2025

NCWIT and PEO STAR

I won the NCWIT Aspirations in Computing Connecticut State Award two years in a row and the PEO STAR Scholarship.

What was hardWriting applications about myself. It felt like bragging, and I almost didn't apply.
What I learnedYou can't get things you don't apply for. This is the first time I learned to just go for it.
Freshman year

Retaking Calculus II

I had to retake Calc II.

What was hardThe first time, I didn't go to office hours. I thought I could figure it out on my own.
What I learnedThe second time I went to office hours every week, and it made a huge difference. Asking for help isn't a weakness. It's part of doing the work.
2026

Eye in the Sky, Travelers AI Research Fellowship

I worked on Eye in the Sky, a proposal for Travelers that scores hail damage on individual properties using clear, rules-based scoring, then uses AI on aerial images to double-check it and catch edited or AI-generated claim photos.

What was hardIt was way past anything from my classes. I had to learn machine learning and computer vision mostly on my own while the project was already moving.
What I learnedMy CS classes are the starting point, not the whole thing. Going deeper on my own is what got me ready for this, and this fellowship is what led to my internship.
See the Eye in the Sky site →
Summer 2026

Front-end lead on Therapify, Cigna practicum

I was the front-end lead on Therapify, a project in the Cigna-sponsored practicum at UConn.

What was hardLeading other people for the first time on a real team, keeping everyone's work consistent, and hitting deadlines.
What I learnedCommunication is just as much the job as coding. Checking in early saved us from a lot of problems later.
2026 to now

Research with Dr. Yuyang Wang

I'm doing undergraduate research with Dr. Yuyang Wang on integrated circuits for AR and VR.

What was hardComing from CS, circuits and hardware felt like a whole new language. I'm usually the person in the room who knows the least.
What I learnedThat's okay. Asking questions and reading a lot is how I'm catching up, and it's showing me a side of engineering I wouldn't have seen in my CS classes.
Every week

Working while commuting

I'm a commuter student, and I work as an IT technician at the UConn Foundation and as a tutor at Mathnasium.

What was hardTime. Between the commute, work, and classes, there aren't a lot of free hours.
What I learnedI plan my week ahead of time so I know where everything fits. Work also taught me things classes don't, like explaining things clearly and staying calm when something breaks.
Now

LeetCode, Theta Tau, and Travelers

I've been doing LeetCode regularly, I joined the Theta Tau engineering fraternity, and I accepted a software engineering internship at Travelers.

What was hardLeetCode was humbling at first. A lot of problems I couldn't solve without looking at hints.
What I learnedI'm getting better because I keep doing it. And I'm going for more opportunities because I learned to just apply.
